One of the most common questions we get from Westmoreland County homeowners is simple: "Does this tree need to come down, or can it just be trimmed?"

It's a smart question — and the answer matters. Tree removal is significantly more expensive than trimming, and unnecessary removal eliminates a valuable asset from your property. On the other hand, trimming a tree that should be removed creates a false sense of security while the real problem continues to develop underground or inside the trunk.

Here's how to think through the decision — and what professionals look at when making the call.

![Arborist climbing and trimming a large oak tree in a residential yard](https://picsum.photos/id/37/800/500)

## What Is Tree Trimming (and When Does It Work)?

Tree trimming — also called tree pruning — removes specific branches to achieve a goal. Depending on what's being removed and why, trimming can:

- **Eliminate hazards** by removing dead, diseased, or structurally weak branches before they fall
- **Improve structure** by removing crossing branches, water sprouts, and co-dominant leaders that could split
- **Increase light and air circulation** through the canopy, which reduces disease pressure and improves the lawn beneath
- **Maintain clearance** from structures, power lines, and rooflines
- **Shape and aesthetics** for ornamental trees and specimen plantings

Trimming is the right call when the tree's fundamental structure — its trunk and main scaffold branches — is sound. Even a tree with significant deadwood, storm damage, or pest pressure can often be preserved with the right trimming approach.

### Types of Pruning Cuts

Not all tree trimming is the same. Professional arborists use specific techniques:

- **Crown cleaning** — removes dead, dying, diseased, and crossing branches from the entire canopy
- **Crown thinning** — selectively removes interior branches to increase light penetration and airflow
- **Crown raising** — removes lower branches to provide clearance above a structure, roadway, or lawn area
- **Crown reduction** — reduces the overall size of the canopy while maintaining the tree's natural shape

## What Triggers Tree Removal?

Tree removal becomes the right answer when the tree cannot be made safe, cannot recover, or when the risk to people and structures outweighs the benefit of keeping it.

**Removal is typically recommended when:**

### The Trunk Has Extensive Internal Decay
Unlike branch-level decay, trunk decay compromises the structural column that holds the tree up. An arborist can probe or drill to assess how much sound wood remains. The general threshold: if more than one-third of the trunk's circumference or cross-section is hollow or decayed, removal is typically indicated.

### Root System Is Severely Damaged or Failing
The root system is invisible — and often the source of the problem. Signs of root failure include:
- Sudden severe leaning
- Heaving soil around the base
- Fungal growth at the soil line (indicating root rot)
- History of nearby construction, excavation, or grade changes

A tree with a failing root system is unpredictable and cannot be stabilized by trimming.

### More Than 50% of the Canopy Is Dead or Diseased
When a majority of a tree's canopy is gone, the tree typically doesn't have the photosynthetic capacity to sustain itself — even if the disease or pest damage is treated. Removal and replanting with a healthy tree is usually the better long-term outcome.

### The Tree Is an Invasive or Pest-Spreading Species
Some trees should be removed regardless of their health — invasive species like Tree-of-Heaven (*Ailanthus altissima*) spread aggressively and crowd out native plants. Diseased Ash trees impacted by Emerald Ash Borer — which is widespread in Westmoreland County — may need removal to prevent spread to neighboring healthy Ash trees.

### Location Makes the Tree Irreparably Hazardous
Sometimes a tree is in the wrong place. A healthy tree growing too close to a foundation, directly beneath power lines, or in a location where proper root space will never exist may need to be removed and replanted in an appropriate location.

## The Role of Stump Grinding

Tree removal leaves a stump — and most homeowners want that stump gone. Stump grinding uses a specialized machine to grind the stump and surface roots down several inches below grade.

**Benefits of stump grinding:**
- Eliminates a tripping hazard and mowing obstacle
- Removes a source of wood-decaying fungi that can spread to other trees
- Allows you to plant grass, install landscaping, or build over the area
- Prevents certain species (Locust, Sumac, Aspen) from resprouting from the stump

Stump grinding is typically priced separately from tree removal and is almost always worth adding on. A stump left in place tends to become a bigger eyesore every year.

## Cost Comparison: Trimming vs. Removal

Pricing for tree work in Westmoreland County varies based on tree size, access, and proximity to structures. General ranges:

| Service | Typical Range |
|---------|--------------|
| Tree trimming (small tree, under 30 ft) | $200 – $450 |
| Tree trimming (large tree, over 50 ft) | $600 – $1,200 |
| Tree removal (small tree) | $400 – $800 |
| Tree removal (large or hazardous tree) | $1,200 – $3,000+ |
| Stump grinding (per stump) | $100 – $350 |

The wide range reflects real variables — a 60-foot oak in an open yard costs far less to remove than a 40-foot tree wedged between a house and a fence with limited equipment access.

## Do-It-Yourself Tree Work: When to Say No

Homeowners regularly ask whether small trimming jobs can be DIY. For very small ornamental trees — pruning shrubs, removing branches you can reach from the ground with a handsaw — the answer is often yes.

But for anything involving:
- Work at height (ladders or climbing)
- Chain saw operation
- Proximity to structures or power lines
- Any tree showing signs of structural compromise

The answer is no. Tree work is consistently ranked among the most dangerous occupations in the country. Falling branches are unpredictable, falling from a ladder is catastrophic, and a chain saw is unforgiving of mistakes. The cost of a professional tree service is always less than a hospital bill — or a homeowner's insurance claim.
