## Why Local Experience in Westmoreland County Matters

Not all contractors are created equal — and hiring local makes a real difference. A contractor who has worked extensively in Westmoreland County, PA understands:

- **Local building codes and permit requirements** specific to municipalities like Greensburg, Jeannette, Latrobe, and New Kensington
- **Regional climate conditions** that affect material selection (Pennsylvania winters are hard on exterior work)
- **Established relationships** with local inspectors, subcontractors, and suppliers that keep your project moving
- **Accountability** — a local business has a reputation in your community to protect

When you hire someone from out of the area, you risk working with a contractor who disappears after the job is done and can't be held accountable.

## 5 Questions to Ask Before Hiring Any Contractor

### 1. Are You Licensed and Insured in Pennsylvania?
Always verify that your contractor holds a valid Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration. Ask to see proof of general liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage. If a worker is injured on your property and the contractor isn't insured, you could be liable.

### 2. Can You Provide Local References?
Request at least three references from projects completed in Westmoreland County within the last two years. Call them. Ask about communication, timeline, budget adherence, and whether they'd hire the contractor again.

### 3. What Does Your Written Estimate Include?
A professional contractor provides a detailed written estimate — not a verbal ballpark. The estimate should itemize labor, materials, permits, cleanup, and a payment schedule. Vague estimates lead to surprise charges.

### 4. Who Will Be On My Property Daily?
Find out whether the contractor does the work themselves or subcontracts everything. There's nothing wrong with subcontracting, but you should know who will be on your property and that those subs are also properly insured.

### 5. What Is the Payment Schedule?
Never pay the full amount upfront. A standard and fair structure is roughly 10–30% down, progress payments tied to project milestones, and the final payment only after you've inspected and approved the completed work.

## Red Flags to Watch For

![Warning signs during contractor negotiations](https://picsum.photos/id/160/800/500)

Protect yourself by watching for these common contractor warning signs:

- **Pressure to sign quickly** or accept a "today only" discount
- **Cash-only requests** with no paper trail
- **No physical business address** — just a phone number
- **Unusually low bids** that seem too good to be true (they usually are)
- **Reluctance to pull permits** — permits protect you, not just the contractor
- **Asking you to get the permits yourself** to avoid accountability

## What a Great General Contractor Brings to Your Project

A skilled general contractor does far more than swing a hammer. They serve as your project manager — coordinating schedules, managing subcontractors, solving problems before they become expensive, and keeping the job site safe and organized.

The best contractors also communicate proactively. You should never have to wonder what's happening on your own property. Expect regular updates, clear answers to your questions, and transparency when something unexpected comes up.
