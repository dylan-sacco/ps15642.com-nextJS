A lush, green lawn is one of the most satisfying features of a well-kept property — and one of the most misunderstood. Most homeowners either do too little (the lawn suffers) or do too much at the wrong time (causing damage). The key is a consistent, season-appropriate care schedule.

This guide is written specifically for **Westmoreland County, PA** and the broader Western Pennsylvania climate. Our region falls in USDA Hardiness Zones 5b–6b, with cool-season grasses like Kentucky Bluegrass, Tall Fescue, and Perennial Ryegrass being the dominant lawn types. The timing and approach below is calibrated for those conditions.

## Spring Lawn Care (March – May)

Spring is the most important season for getting your lawn on the right track. But many homeowners start too aggressively, too early.

### March: Wake Up Slowly
- **Soil temperature check** — don't apply any fertilizer until soil temps consistently reach 55°F (use a soil thermometer or check your local extension office data)
- **Light raking** to remove matted leaves, debris, and thatch buildup from winter
- **Assess winter damage** — look for dead patches, snow mold, or vole tunnels and plan repairs

### April: First Applications
- **Pre-emergent herbicide** — apply when soil temps hit 50°F to stop crabgrass and other annual weeds before they germinate. Timing is critical — too late and it won't work
- **First mowing** — begin when grass actively grows, cutting at 3 to 3.5 inches. Never cut more than one-third of the blade at once
- **Spot-treat bare areas** with grass seed matched to your existing lawn type

### May: Full Growth Mode
- **Starter fertilizer** for any overseeded areas
- **Broadleaf weed control** for dandelions, clover, and plantain that slipped through pre-emergent
- Mow regularly at **3–4 inches** — taller grass shades the soil, retaining moisture and crowding out weeds

## Summer Lawn Care (June – August)

Summer in Western PA brings heat stress, drought, and pressure from weeds and insects. The goal in summer is to protect what you've built in spring.

### Mowing in Summer
- **Raise your mowing height to 4 inches** in July and August — taller grass is more heat and drought tolerant
- **Leave clippings on the lawn** — they decompose quickly and return nitrogen to the soil (this is called "grasscycling")
- **Sharpen your mower blade** at least once per season — dull blades tear grass instead of cutting it, making it more susceptible to disease

### Watering the Right Way
- Lawns need approximately **1 inch of water per week** including rainfall
- Water **deeply and infrequently** — one to two times per week, early in the morning — rather than light daily watering that promotes shallow roots
- Let the lawn go dormant naturally during extreme dry spells rather than stressing it with inconsistent watering

### Summer Pest Watch
- **Grub damage** typically becomes visible in late July through August as brown patches that peel up like carpet
- Apply **grub control** in June or early July (preventive) for best results
- Watch for **chinch bugs** and **sod webworms** in drought-stressed areas

## Fall Lawn Care (September – November)

Fall is the **most important season for lawn renovation** in Western PA. Cool-season grasses thrive in fall temperatures, making it the ideal window for aeration, overseeding, and fertilization.

### September: Core Aeration and Overseeding
This is the single highest-return activity you can do for your lawn. Core aeration:
- Relieves soil compaction (critical in our clay-heavy Western PA soils)
- Opens channels for air, water, and nutrients to reach roots
- Creates ideal conditions for seed-to-soil contact when overseeding immediately after

Overseed with a quality **tall fescue or Kentucky bluegrass blend** appropriate for your sun conditions. Fall germination gives new grass time to establish before winter without competing with summer weeds.

### October: Fall Fertilization
Apply a **slow-release, high-potassium fall fertilizer** (often called "winterizer") to build root reserves and strengthen the lawn heading into dormancy. This is the most critical fertilizer application of the year for winter hardiness and spring green-up.

### November: Final Prep
- Complete your **final mowing** at 2.5–3 inches (slightly shorter than summer to prevent snow mold)
- **Leaf removal** is critical — a thick layer of leaves left on the lawn through winter will smother and kill the grass beneath
- Avoid walking on frozen or frost-covered grass, which breaks the grass blades

## Winter Lawn Care (December – February)

Your lawn is dormant but not dead. Keep these things in mind:

- **Minimize traffic** on frozen or dormant grass
- **Salt damage** from ice melt products can kill grass along driveways and walkways — use calcium chloride instead of rock salt where possible, and rinse affected areas in early spring
- **Plan ahead** — order grass seed, fertilizer, and pre-emergent now for spring; plan any renovation projects
