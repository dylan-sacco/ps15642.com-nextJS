<h2 id="deck-construction">Deck Construction</h2>

We handle decks from the ground up — design, permits, footings, framing, and finish. Every project starts with an on-site visit where we measure the space, talk through how you plan to use it, and figure out what the layout should look like. Some homeowners want something minimal: a clean platform off the back door, a few steps down to the yard. Others want more — built-in seating, a pergola overhead, multiple levels, or a wraparound design that follows the shape of the house. We've done all of it.

What sets a deck apart long-term is what you don't see once it's finished: the footings dug below the frost line, the ledger board properly flashed and bolted to the house, the joist hangers and hardware rated for the load. We don't cut corners on the structural side because that's where decks fail. The cosmetic stuff is easy — the bones are what matter.

We also do deck replacement and repairs. If your existing deck has rotting boards, a failing ledger, or railings that no longer meet code, we can assess whether a repair is practical or whether it makes more sense to start fresh. We'll give you an honest answer either way.

<h2 id="material-options">Material Options</h2>

Choosing the right decking material comes down to your budget, how much maintenance you want to do, and what you want it to look like. Here's how we think about the main options:

**Pressure-Treated Lumber**
The most common and cost-effective choice. Modern pressure-treated wood is rated for ground contact and direct exposure, and it holds up well in Western PA's freeze-thaw cycles when installed correctly. It does require periodic sealing or staining to maintain appearance and prevent cracking. If you're looking for maximum square footage per dollar, this is typically the best route.

**Composite Decking**
Brands like Trex, TimberTech, and Fiberon offer composite boards that resist fading, staining, and splintering without the annual maintenance of wood. They cost more upfront but tend to pay for themselves over time. Most composites come with 25–30 year warranties and are available in a wide range of colors and textures. If low maintenance is a priority, this is what we recommend.

**Cedar**
A natural wood option that sits between pressure-treated and composite in terms of cost. Cedar has natural oils that resist rot and insects, and it looks great when freshly stained. It's softer than pressure-treated, so it's prone to denting in high-traffic areas, but many homeowners prefer the natural aesthetic. Cedar is a good choice when appearance is the primary driver.

<h2 id="aluminum-railings">Aluminum Railings</h2>

We install aluminum railing systems on decks, porches, and exterior stairs. Aluminum has become the default choice for most projects — it doesn't rot, doesn't need painting, doesn't rust, and holds up through Western PA winters without issue. Most systems are powder-coated and available in standard colors (black, white, bronze) that can match almost any exterior.

Aluminum railing installation isn't just about bolting posts to the frame. Proper post spacing, correct fastener torque, and secure top and bottom rail connections all affect whether the railing passes inspection and whether it actually feels solid when you lean on it. We've seen a lot of railing jobs that look fine from a distance but flex under load — that's not acceptable on a deck.

We also do railing replacements on existing decks. If your current wood or iron railings are failing, rotting, or just outdated, we can pull them and install a clean aluminum system without touching the rest of the deck. That's a common job and it makes a significant visual difference.

<h2 id="permits--code-compliance">Permits & Code Compliance</h2>

Most deck additions in Westmoreland County municipalities require a building permit, especially when the deck is attached to the structure or elevated above a certain height. Requirements vary by municipality — some require engineered drawings, others just need a site plan and material list.

We'll tell you upfront what your municipality requires so there are no surprises mid-project. If a permit is needed, we help navigate that process. Everything we build is constructed to meet or exceed the IRC residential deck construction guidelines, including ledger attachment, footing depth, post sizing, and railing height requirements.
