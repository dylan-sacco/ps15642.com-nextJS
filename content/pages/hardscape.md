<h2 id="concrete-patios">Concrete Patios</h2>

A poured concrete patio is one of the more practical investments you can make in a backyard. It's durable, low-maintenance, and gives you a solid, level surface that holds up for decades when properly installed. We do both standard broom-finish concrete and decorative stamped patterns if you want something with more character.

The prep work is where most patio jobs succeed or fail. Before a single yard of concrete gets poured, we excavate to proper depth, compact the subbase, and install adequate gravel to manage drainage and reduce frost heave. Concrete that's poured over unstable or poorly prepared ground will crack — it's not a question of if, it's when. We don't skip that step.

We control-joint every slab at appropriate intervals so the concrete has somewhere to move without forming random cracks across the surface. Sizing and layout get confirmed on-site before we start — we'll mark it out, walk you through it, and make sure it connects to the house and yard the way you're picturing. Concrete is permanent, so we want you confident in the layout before we commit.

We can accommodate requests for colored concrete, exposed aggregate finishes, and border work. If you've seen something you like and want to match it, bring a photo and we'll tell you whether it's achievable within the budget.

<h2 id="sidewalks--walkways">Sidewalks & Walkways</h2>

Cracked or heaving concrete is a trip hazard and it looks rough — especially on a property that's otherwise well-kept. We do both new installations and replacements, and we try to avoid unnecessary demolition when it isn't needed.

If only a few sections of an existing walk are damaged, we can saw-cut and pull just those panels and pour replacements that blend with the existing work. You don't always need to tear up a whole sidewalk to fix it. We'll tell you honestly whether a targeted repair makes sense or whether the whole thing is too far gone to be worth patching.

For new walkways, we think about more than just the path itself — how it grades away from the house, how it ties into steps or a porch, whether there's adequate room on either side for landscaping. A walkway that drains toward the foundation creates problems. We get the grading right.

We use expansion joints at transitions and wherever the walk meets another structure. Fresh mix is delivered and poured same-day — we don't use leftover or remixed concrete.

<h2 id="retaining-walls">Retaining Walls</h2>

Retaining walls solve real problems: eroding hillsides, unusable sloped yards, soil creep toward the house or driveway, and drainage issues that pile up over time. We build walls using concrete block, natural stone, and poured concrete depending on the height of the wall, the load behind it, and the aesthetic you're going for.

Wall construction isn't just stacking material. Anything over a certain height needs a proper drainage layer behind it to relieve hydrostatic pressure — without that, even a well-built wall will eventually bow or fail. We install filter fabric and drainage aggregate as part of the build, not as an afterthought.

For taller walls or walls holding back significant loads (driveways, upper-level structures), we'll also talk through whether an engineered design makes sense. We'd rather tell you upfront than build something undersized.

<h2 id="drainage-solutions">Drainage Solutions</h2>

Standing water, wet basements, and soggy yards are almost always a drainage problem — and drainage problems rarely fix themselves. We install French drains, catch basins, and surface grading corrections to redirect water away from structures and off the property.

A French drain is a gravel-filled trench with a perforated pipe running through it. It intercepts groundwater or surface runoff at a high point and carries it to a lower outlet — a daylight exit in the yard, a storm drain connection, or a dry creek bed. It's one of the most effective ways to deal with persistent wet areas, and it doesn't require tearing up a finished yard to install.

We also do downspout extensions, swale grading, and yard regrading for properties where the original grade directs water toward the house. If you've had water in a basement or crawl space, a lot of the time the fix starts outside — we can walk the property with you and explain what we're seeing.
