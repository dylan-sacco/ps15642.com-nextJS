<h2 id="removal--cleanup">Removal & Cleanup</h2>

Before anything new goes in, the old stuff has to come out. We handle shrub and small tree removal, overgrown bed cleanup, invasive plant removal, and whatever else is in the way. We haul everything off the property and leave the area graded, clean, and ready for the next phase.

We're also used to dealing with the aftermath of neglect — beds that haven't been touched in years, volunteer trees that have taken over, root systems that spread farther than expected. None of that surprises us. We work efficiently and we don't leave a mess behind.

If you're not sure what to keep and what to take out, we can walk the property with you and give recommendations. Sometimes mature plantings are worth saving with some pruning and reshaping; sometimes they're too far gone. We'll give you an honest read either way.

<h2 id="landscape-design">Landscape Design</h2>

Good landscape design starts with understanding how the property is actually used — where people walk, where they want shade, what views they want to frame or block, what the drainage situation is, and what level of maintenance the owner is realistically going to do. A beautiful design that requires two hours of work a week is useless to a homeowner who has an hour a month.

We design around Western PA's climate specifically. That means selecting plants that handle our freeze-thaw cycles, our clay-heavy soils, and the microclimates created by your specific site. A south-facing slope with full sun needs completely different plant choices than a shaded north-facing foundation planting. We factor all of that in.

Our designs typically combine:

- A mix of perennials and shrubs that return each year without replanting
- Seasonal color annuals in key spots where visual impact matters most
- Structural evergreens that provide year-round form and privacy
- Ground covers in areas where grass struggles to establish

We'll walk you through the design before any plants are ordered, so you understand what you're getting and why each choice was made.

<h2 id="planting--installation">Planting & Installation</h2>

Our installation work goes beyond digging holes and dropping plants in. Proper planting depth, soil amendment where needed, and the right spacing for mature growth size all matter significantly for long-term health. Plants that are set too deep, too shallow, or too close together fail earlier than they should — and that costs the homeowner money.

We source quality plant material from regional nurseries and growers, and we'll tell you if we don't have confidence in a particular species performing well on your site. We'd rather recommend something that'll thrive than upsell you on something that looks great at install and declines over the next two seasons.

Installation includes:

- Shrubs and ornamental trees
- Perennial beds and mixed plantings
- Seasonal color (annuals for spring, summer, and fall)
- Native and pollinator-friendly plantings
- Transplanting existing specimens to better locations

<h2 id="softscaping--plant-beds">Softscaping & Plant Beds</h2>

Softscaping refers to all the organic elements of a landscape — the beds, plantings, and groundcovers that give a yard its character and structure. A well-designed plant bed does more than look good: it reduces mowing, controls erosion, moderates soil temperature, and gives the overall landscape a finished, intentional appearance.

We cut clean bed edges that separate planting areas from lawn, install weed barrier fabric where appropriate (not everywhere — it depends on the planting type), and build beds with enough depth and quality soil to give roots room to establish. Shallow, compacted beds are one of the most common reasons plants underperform.

For foundation plantings specifically, we think carefully about mature size. Shrubs that are tight against the house are almost always ones that were planted without accounting for how large they'd grow — and they cause moisture problems and damage siding. We plant with the final size in mind, which means initial spacing looks open for the first year or two but fills in correctly over time.

<h2 id="mulching--edging">Mulching & Edging</h2>

Mulch does a lot of practical work: it suppresses weeds, retains soil moisture, moderates temperature at the root zone, and improves the look of any bed. But it's often done wrong — piled too deep against plant stems and tree trunks, or applied over compacted soil without any prep.

We apply mulch at the right depth (typically 2–3 inches), keep it pulled away from stems and trunks, and don't reapply over existing thick layers without removing some of what's already there. Volcano mulching around trees is a common landscaping mistake we don't make — it causes decay and long-term damage.

We also cut sharp, clean bed edges that give the whole property a finished look. Edging is one of those details that's easy to skip and makes an enormous visual difference.

<h2 id="outdoor-lighting">Outdoor Lighting</h2>

We install landscape lighting for pathways, planting beds, trees, steps, and outdoor living areas. Most installations are low-voltage LED systems — they're efficient, reliable, and create a warm, natural-looking effect that's very different from harsh floodlights.

Good lighting serves two purposes: it extends the usability of outdoor spaces into the evening, and it adds a layer of safety to stairs and walkways that are otherwise easy to miss in the dark. Both matter.

We design lighting placement to highlight the landscape rather than overwhelm it. The goal is to make the plantings and hardscape look better after sunset — not to turn the yard into a parking lot. We'll talk through fixture placement with you before installation so you can visualize the effect.
