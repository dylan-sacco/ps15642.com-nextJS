<h2 id="tree-removal">Tree Removal</h2>

Tree removal is one of those jobs where experience and proper equipment really matter. A tree in a wide-open field is straightforward. Most residential trees aren't — they're growing close to houses, over fences, near power lines, or in spots where a bad cut sends wood somewhere it can't go. We're used to working in those conditions.

Our approach to removal depends on the site. Large trees close to structures are typically sectioned from the top down, with each piece rigged and lowered in a controlled way rather than dropped. Trees in more open areas can often be felled in sections more quickly. We assess each job on-site and work the safest, most efficient plan for the specific situation.

Cleanup is part of the job. We chip branches on-site and haul out the wood. When we leave, the yard is clean — not just "the tree is down." We also rake and blow out wood chips from the surrounding area before we go.

We take on:

- Dead or dying trees
- Trees leaning toward structures, fences, or vehicles
- Trees with significant storm damage or structural failure
- Overgrown trees with root systems affecting foundations or driveways
- Trees that have simply outgrown the space

<h2 id="trimming--pruning">Trimming & Pruning</h2>

Regular trimming and pruning keeps trees healthier, safer, and better-looking over the long run. A properly pruned tree is more structurally sound in high wind, less likely to drop large limbs unexpectedly, and less likely to cause damage to the house, driveway, or neighboring property.

There's a difference between trimming and pruning, and we do both. Trimming is primarily aesthetic — shaping the canopy, cleaning up the silhouette, pulling limbs away from the roofline or gutters. Pruning is structural — removing dead, diseased, or crossing branches, correcting poor branch angles that create weak attachment points, and thinning the canopy to reduce wind load.

Done correctly, pruning extends the life of a tree significantly. Done incorrectly — or done with the wrong tools at the wrong time of year — it opens the tree to disease and decay. We prune to the branch collar, not flush cuts, and we don't use wound sealants (research consistently shows they do more harm than good).

We work on:

- Shade trees, ornamental trees, and fruit trees
- Trees with limbs over roofs, decks, or power lines
- Trees with dead wood, crossing branches, or poor structure
- Young trees being trained for long-term form
- Mature trees that haven't been touched in years

<h2 id="stump-grinding">Stump Grinding</h2>

After a tree comes down, the stump is what's left — and most people live with them longer than they should. Stumps are trip hazards, they attract wood-boring insects, and over time the decaying root system can create sinkholes and uneven ground. They also just look bad in an otherwise clean yard.

We grind stumps down below grade — typically 8–12 inches depending on your plans for the area. Once ground, you can seed over it, lay sod, plant a shrub, or install hardscape. The wood chip debris left behind is often workable as mulch, or we can haul it off.

A few things worth knowing:

- **We grind stumps from previous removals**, not just trees we cut. If you have old stumps on the property from work done years ago, we can take care of those too.
- **Root systems remain underground** after grinding — we're grinding the stump itself, not excavating roots. In most cases this is fine; roots decompose over time. If roots need to be fully removed (for foundation work or a new structure), we can discuss that separately.
- **Grinding depth matters.** A stump ground only 2–3 inches deep will re-sprout from species that regenerate from roots. We go deep enough to prevent that.

<h2 id="storm-damage-cleanup">Storm Damage Cleanup</h2>

When a major storm comes through, downed trees and large limbs need to be dealt with quickly — not just for aesthetics, but because wood on a roof, a fence, or near the foundation can cause ongoing damage. We try to respond to storm calls as fast as possible.

Storm cleanup often involves:

- Removing fallen trees or large limbs from structures, vehicles, or the yard
- Cutting and removing trees that partially fell and are leaning on something
- Cleaning up debris and wood from the affected area
- Assessing remaining trees on the property for new hazards created by the storm

If a tree has come down on your property after a storm and you need someone out quickly, give us a call directly.

<h2 id="tree-health--hazard-assessment">Tree Health & Hazard Assessment</h2>

Not every tree problem is obvious from the ground. A tree that looks fine from a distance can have significant interior decay, a compromised root system, or structural defects that make it a risk in a storm. Signs that a tree may need professional attention include:

- Cracks or splits in the trunk or major limbs
- Fungi or mushrooms growing at the base (a sign of root rot)
- Leaning that has developed recently or after a storm
- Deadwood in the canopy — especially large, central branches
- Bark abnormalities, including deep cracks, missing bark sections, or weeping areas
- Hollow sections visible at the base or in crotches

We can walk the property with you and point out what we're seeing. Sometimes a tree just needs a crown cleaning or some structural pruning. Sometimes the honest answer is that it's a hazard and it needs to come down. Either way, we'll give you a straight read — we're not in the business of recommending unnecessary removals.
